# T1-E3.10 - MULTI-worthy triage flag + batch pause/yield

> **Origin:** The batch presets force `AUTO`+`SINGLE` on every queued plan regardless of complexity, so a `MULTI`-worthy plan can run with no independent reviewer and no adversarial pass. This parcel flags such plans at initiation, lets the operator accept the risk or defer, and adds a pause/yield so a deferred plan is delivered independently by the user. Wave 4 of the batch-hardening sequence.

## 1 Phase 1: Expansion & Scoping
**Skill Executed:** `ptp-context-hunter`
**Intent:**
Flag `MULTI`-worthy plans **before** an unattended batch claims them, let the operator accept the batch risk or defer, and make a deferral a **pure pause** at that plan's slot so the plan can be delivered independently (with a real reviewer) instead of silently running `SINGLE`.

**In Scope:**
- A **declared** triage recommendation in the plan's claim front-matter (`triage: MULTI | SINGLE`), recorded at commit time by `@sprint-plan` § 4c from the canonical five signals in `@pass-the-parcel` § Agent Topology → Complexity Triage (cited, never restated).
- A **mechanical** backstop so the flag cannot be lost to an optimistic or unedited declaration: `scripts/sprint_eligible.py` computes `blast-radius` from the plan's own declared `touches` (the canonical low bound is "≤ 3 files, one domain"), and a plan is `multi_worthy` when **either** the declared triage **or** a mechanical signal fires.
- `sprint-run` § 1 runs that script for its forecast (never "on paper") and presents the per-flagged-plan fork in the existing informed preview: **accept batch risk** (`AUTO` + `SINGLE`, no independent reviewer) or **defer to manual**.
- The yield: a deferral is a **pure pause at the deferred plan's slot** — the batch never claims past it — reported as `DEFERRED-MANUAL` with the topology the plan needs, the dependents it blocks, and the resume contract.
- `ptp-parcel-fast` records the flag at claim time, so a plan that ran `SINGLE` under the locked preset while flagged `MULTI` says so in its own Plan Settings block instead of leaving the risk invisible at Gate D.
- The canonical front-matter list in **every** mirror (prefix + seed + 9 inlined agents + `template-plan.md` + `plan-lifecycle.md` + `pass-the-parcel`).

**Out of Scope:**
- Hard-skipping `MULTI` plans with a recorded reason (rejected — a soft fork with operator choice is the chosen design).
- **Pause-and-advance** (run the plans after the hole in the same invocation) — needs a scheduler; that is `T1-E3.11`'s territory. The `ponytail:` ceiling below records it.
- Auto-classifying complexity mid-run once Phases 1–5 have produced real data.
- A persisted deferral state on the plan file (a deferral is an in-invocation decision; the next invocation re-derives it from live state).
- Gate D semantics; the consolidated batched verdict remains.
- Any change to `parcel-sprint`'s `opencode.json` `permission.task` allow-list (no new spawn target is introduced).

**Perimeter Notes:**
- The pre-run flag is a **guess** — the plan has not been through the phases that would reveal its true complexity. The human decides; the flag only advises.
- The fork must resolve **before** the first claim, or the plan is already `CLAIMED` and no longer claimable by the user's independent run.
- "Accept the risk" must be pre-clearable per plan (and for all), so a fully unattended run stays possible; the pause fires only for plans deliberately deferred.
- Dependency interaction: ship **pure pause** first (stop the line); pause-and-advance is a later refinement.
- `ponytail:` ceiling — pure pause means plans *after* the deferred one in `claim_order` do not run in that invocation, even when they are disjoint from it. Upgrade path = pause-and-advance, which is a scheduler and belongs to `T1-E3.11`.

**Q3 — Auto-Resolution:** **Pure pause, fired at the deferred plan's slot.** The batch claims and runs in `claim_order` as normal, but it **never claims past** a deferred plan: on reaching that plan's position it halts, emits the partial report, and records the `DEFERRED-MANUAL` yield. No scheduler, no hop.
**Rationale:** stopping *at* the hole rather than *before* the line throws away nothing that is already claimable while keeping the queue-order determinism the fixpoint is built on (the loop must not silently hop a hole, or a declared intent becomes an execution surprise). Pause-and-advance is a genuine scheduler and `T1-E3.11` owns that surface; the operator's lever against a pure pause being harsh is Q4's pre-acceptance — a plan that is *not* deferred never pauses anything.
**Source:** `.devops/rules/plan-lifecycle.md` § Claim Protocol (fixpoint: bounded / deterministic / cycle-safe / progress-correct); `.devops/sprints/sprint-9-batch-hardening/sprint.md` § Delivery Model (six serial waves)

**Source:** `.devops/skills/sprint-run/SKILL.md` § 1 step 7 (*"then take **one** yes/no"*, *"Decline → no claims, no writes"*); `.devops/agents/parcel-sprint.agent.md` § Workflow step 3

**Q5 — Auto-Resolution:** **Resume = a plain re-invocation of `@sprint-run`.** It re-runs § 1 preflight in full and re-applies § 2 to live state; **no deferral state is persisted anywhere**, so the fork is recomputed from the plan files each time.

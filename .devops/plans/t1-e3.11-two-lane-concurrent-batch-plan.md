## 1 Phase 1: Expansion & Scoping
**Skill Executed:** `ptp-context-hunter`
**Intent:**
Run disjoint plans concurrently in isolated worktrees while serialising shared-machinery surfaces in a serial lane — and move the `machinery-version` bump + prefix `-Sync` to the host/wrap-up so machinery plans stop colliding on shared counters.

**In Scope:**
- Reserved-surface classification: lane A (serial) when `touches` intersects the shared/global set; lane B (parallel) otherwise.
- Parallel groups computed from T1-E3.09's group output; worktree + `plan/<code>-<slug>` branch per lane-B plan; concurrent spawn; **serialised merge** back to trunk in queue order.
- Version-bump ownership: `machinery-version` + its `version-history.md` literal + the prefix `-Sync` move out of individual plans into the host/batch wrap-up (one bump per batch).
- Segment-wise glob overlap (upgrades the prefix-or-equal heuristic that becomes a correctness risk under concurrency).
- `plan-lifecycle.md` § Deviations: `trunk-sequential` replaced by `group-parallel`.

**Out of Scope:**
- A full independent-set scheduler beyond two lanes.
- Relaxing the `touches` predicate or the batched Gate D.
- Concurrency as a cost lever — it is wall-clock only and may cost slightly more in re-verification.

**Perimeter Notes:**
- In **this** template nearly every authoritative plan touches machinery, so lane B is thin here; the payoff is in satellites with a disjoint `src/**`. State that honestly rather than selling speed the template won't see.
- `touches` fidelity stops being scheduling-only and becomes correctness: a mis-declared write set becomes a merge conflict or a lost edit.
- Shared surfaces (`sync-manifest.yaml`, `version-history.md`, `agent-changelog.md`, `sprint.md`, `backlog-index.md`, `.devops/agents/**` + `base-context.md`) remain trunk-only and host-owned.

**Phase 3.5 auto-resolutions (`AUTO` mode — `ptp-phase3-answerer`):**

**Q1 — Reserved-surface set.**
`Auto-Resolution:` The set is defined by one test — *is the write global rather than file-local?* — and is stated canonically in `.devops/rules/plan-lifecycle.md` § Claim Protocol → *Reserved Surfaces & the Lane Model*: `.opencode/plans/base-context.md`, `.devops/templates/base-context.template.md`, `.devops/agents/**`, `.devops/sync-manifest.yaml`, `.devops/logs/version-history.md`, `.devops/logs/agent-changelog.md`, `.devops/sprints/**`, `.devops/backlog/backlog-index.md`, `.devops/backlog/SPRINTS.md`. A plan hitting **any** of them is on the serial lane. Plus one **transitive** rule: a `.devops/skills/<slug>/SKILL.md` edit whose body is embedded in an existing `.devops/agents/<slug>.subagent.md` is also serial, because executing it forces a `-Sync` that rewrites a reserved agent file.
`Rationale:` The counter and the prefix lock are the two surfaces where a concurrent write *corrupts* rather than conflicts; classifying by "is the write global?" keeps the set derivable from rules that already exist (step 6 already makes these trunk-only) instead of inventing a second taxonomy. The transitive rule is not optional padding: this template's most common edits are `ptp-*` skills, so a classifier that misses the embed cascade misclassifies almost the whole queue as lane-B.
`Source:` `.devops/rules/plan-lifecycle.md` § Claim Protocol step 6 + *Write-Set Overlap Predicate*; `scripts/check-parcel-prefix.ps1` (the embed contract + `exit 1` on drift); `.devops/sync-manifest.yaml`.

**Q2 — Where the bump moves; is there a mid-batch CI window?**
`Auto-Resolution:` **Batch wrap-up**, one increment per batch, read **live at that moment** (never a value read earlier). There is no mid-batch CI-failing window to trade: `validate.yml`'s version-discipline step asserts only that the *current* manifest value appears in a `version-history.md` row, and the batch never pushes — the wrap-up reads the value after all claims are done, so it cannot be stale-by-construction.
`Rationale:` `@agent-wrap-up` already owns the bump discipline (Phase 7b) and batch scope already runs every phase but 4 once per batch, so this is one added clause rather than a new mechanism. Reading the value at wrap-up is the actual fix for the collision — the bug was never "who bumps", it was "bumped from a base captured earlier".
`Source:` `.github/workflows/validate.yml` (Machinery version discipline step); `.devops/skills/agent-wrap-up/SKILL.md` § Batch Scope + Phase 7b; `HOW-TO.md` § Versioning.

**Q3 — Prefix `-Sync` ownership; a lane-A plan edits `base-context.md` mid-batch.**
`Auto-Resolution:` **Reasoned reversal of the plan's premise.** The proposed "one regenerate per batch, host-owned" is rejected: deferring the regenerate leaves `check-parcel-prefix.ps1` **red** from the edit until the batch ends, and a red tree is exactly what the next claim's green-baseline preflight (`sprint-run` § 1 step 3) and every per-plan Phase 9 gate refuse. Ownership therefore stays with the plan that made the edit — its `-Sync` runs as part of that edit — and what the **host** owns is the **invariant**: `check-parcel-prefix.ps1` exits `0` at every claim boundary.
`Rationale:` A deferred sync converts a bounded, locally-provable repair into a red window at precisely the moment the predicate's next evaluation needs green. "Green at every claim boundary" is the property the preflight actually consumes; "one regenerate per batch" is a cost optimisation that trades a hard gate for tidiness.
`Source:` `scripts/check-parcel-prefix.ps1` (`exit 1` on prefix or embed drift); `.devops/skills/sprint-run/SKILL.md` § 1 step 3; `.devops/skills/ptp-code-surgeon/SKILL.md` § 5 (Phase 9 QA = every command exits 0).

**Q4 — Failure policy for a HALTed lane-B member.**
`Auto-Resolution:` **Unchanged — a `HALT` stops the batch** (`sprint-run` § 5). The proposed "merge survivors, leave the failed branch" is inoperable and is **not** adopted: under deviation 3 no branch exists and the host never merges or prunes.
`Rationale:` Recovering lane-B survivors requires worktree isolation plus a merge, i.e. a fifth deviation and a host doing something it is structurally prevented from doing. A lane is a classification, not an execution namespace. This costs nothing today, because nothing in this template classifies into lane B anyway.
`Source:` `.devops/rules/plan-lifecycle.md` § Deviations (exactly four, item 3); `.devops/skills/sprint-run/SKILL.md` § 5.

**Q5 — Merge order and re-verification cadence.**
`Auto-Resolution:` **N/A — there is no merge.** Verification stays per-plan at Phase 9 plus one repo-gate pass for the batch at the follow-up batch wrap-up (§ Batch Scope, Phase 7a). The lane classification adds no re-verification pass.
`Rationale:` With no worktree there is nothing to merge, so "once per group vs once per merge" has no referent. The existing one-gate-pass-per-batch is already the cheapest correct answer, and a lane-scoped pass would be verification theatre.
`Source:` `.devops/skills/agent-wrap-up/SKILL.md` § Batch Scope; `.devops/rules/plan-lifecycle.md` § Deviations items 1–3.

**Q6 — Group size cap; do lanes run simultaneously?**
`Auto-Resolution:` **No cap is introduced, and the batch runs lanes serially.** `lanes` and `parallel_groups` stay **advisory** data (the `T1-E3.09` precedent — data for the host, not a clause of the predicate) naming which plans *could* run concurrently under a runner that provides real isolation. `@sprint-run` continues to claim one plan at a time to a fixpoint.
`Rationale:` Two concurrent writers on one trunk working tree is not isolation, it is a race. A cap is meaningless without a scheduler, so it would be YAGNI — and YAGNI is what `ptp-high-visionary`'s Simplicity Ladder rejects. Stating the advisory-only boundary is what keeps the model honest instead of implying a concurrency this path does not have.
`Source:` `scripts/sprint_eligible.py` docstring (`parallel_groups` advisory ceiling, scheduler reserved to this plan); `.devops/skills/sprint-run/SKILL.md` § 2 (fixpoint loop).

**Q7 — Does concurrency change the consolidated Gate D report shape?**
`Auto-Resolution:` Marginally. The consolidated report gains the lane classification (serial lane + lane-B groups) so the human verdict reads the blast radius; no new row type and no new verdict.
`Rationale:` The report is *directed* — its job is to drive the next decision — and lanes are an input to that judgement, not a second result to reconcile. A new row type would imply an execution model the path does not have.
`Source:` `.devops/skills/sprint-run/SKILL.md` § 6.

**Q8 — Interaction with `T1-E3.10`'s yield.**
`Auto-Resolution:` **Confirmed orthogonal.** A deferred plan is a pause *at its slot*: it is never claimed, so it appears in neither `claim_order` nor a lane group; `blocks` independently reports the dependents the deferral strands.
`Rationale:` The yield removes a plan from execution order, not from the dependency graph, and lane/complexity classification is computed over the queued set — two different projections that cannot interact.
`Source:` `.devops/rules/plan-lifecycle.md` § Claim Protocol → *MULTI-worthy Yield*; `scripts/sprint_eligible.py` `blocks_for()` / `compute()`.

**Architectural Conflict Warnings Raised:**
- The version counter is **global and monotonic**; without moving its ownership, concurrent machinery plans collide and fail the CI predicate. This is the prerequisite, not a nice-to-have. — **Resolved (Q2):** one bump per batch, read live, owned by the batch wrap-up.
- `touches` mis-declaration becomes a merge conflict under concurrency — the segment-wise glob upgrade is **required**, not optional. — **Resolved:** the prefix-or-equal ceiling is closed; the matcher becomes segment-wise (Phase 5 step 1).
- **New, raised at execution:** the plan's lane *execution* model (worktree + branch + serialised merge) contradicts live deviation 3 and the host's structural shape (no branch, never merges/prunes). Recorded rather than re-imposed — see Phase 5 § *Reconciliation*. Lanes ship as a classification; concurrent execution is **not** enabled in the batch path.
